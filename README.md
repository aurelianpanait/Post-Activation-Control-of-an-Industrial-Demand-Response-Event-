# Post-Activation Control of an Industrial Demand Response Event

**Médiane 10J baseline reconstruction and compliance settlement for a 200 MW industrial site under the French *contrôle du réalisé* framework.**

> Technical case study — MINES Paris · PSL — April 2026.

---

## Overview

A demand response aggregator manages a portfolio of industrial clients that offer downward flexibility (*effacement de consommation*) to RTE, the French transmission system operator. When an activation order is issued, the aggregator dispatches the curtailment signal to the client's site, and once the activation window has closed, must perform an **ex-post audit** (*contrôle du réalisé*) to verify whether the client actually delivered the requested curtailment.

This repository performs the full post-activation control for a single event on **24 January 2024**: a 50 MW curtailment setpoint applied between 09:00 and 11:00 on Client A, an industrial consumer with a nominal draw of ~200 MW. It answers three questions:

1. What is the counterfactual baseline — i.e. the consumption the client *would have drawn* without the activation?
2. How much energy did the client actually curtail across the 12 ten-minute timesteps of the window?
3. What is the financial settlement (remuneration and penalty) once the realisation rate is compared against the 80% compliance threshold?

## Headline results

| Indicator | Value |
|---|---:|
| Activation period | 09:00 – 11:00 (12 × 10 min) |
| Setpoint | 50 MW |
| Expected curtailed volume | 100.00 MWh |
| **Delivered curtailed volume** | **96.33 MWh** |
| **Realisation rate** | **96.3%** |
| Compliance threshold | 80% |
| **Compliance status** | **COMPLIANT** |
| Remuneration (96.33 MWh × 350 €/MWh) | 33,715.88 € |
| Penalty | 0.00 € |
| **Net payment** | **33,715.88 €** |

**Per-timestep decomposition reveals an asymmetric response:**
- First hour (09:00–10:10): under-delivery, curtailed power between 45.3 and 49.9 MW
- Second hour (10:20–10:50): compliant or over-delivering, curtailed power ≥ 50 MW
- The transition at 10:20 is sharp (45.3 → 49.8 MW) and is consistent with the ramping dynamics of a 200 MW industrial load adjusting to a 25% reduction signal.

The Médiane 10J baseline is remarkably flat at ~200 MW (within ±1 MW), confirming that the 10 reference days (10–23 January 2024) were genuinely normal operating days and that the median aggregation absorbed day-to-day noise effectively.

## Data description

The dataset covers the **whole month of January 2024** at 10-minute resolution (4,464 timesteps). Both CSVs use semicolon (`;`) separator and `DD/MM/YYYY` European date format.

| File | Rows | Columns | Description |
|---|---:|---|---|
| `Consommation_Client_Janvier2024.csv` | 4,464 | Horodate, P Soutiree (kW) | Active power draw of Client A, 10-min resolution |
| `Parametres.csv` | 1 | Client, Debut, Fin, Puissance_consigne_MW, Prix_offre_eur_MWh, Prix_penalites_eur_MWh | Contractual terms of the activation |

**Activation parameters (from `Parametres.csv`):**
- Client: Client A
- Activation window: 24/01/2024 09:00 – 11:00
- Setpoint: 50 MW (i.e. 25% reduction on the daytime load of ~200 MW)
- Offer price: 350 €/MWh on delivered volume
- Penalty price: 250 €/MWh on deficit volume (only applied if realisation < 80%)

**Reference window for the Médiane 10J baseline:**
The 10 most recent business days prior to the activation are: **10, 11, 12, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 22, 23 January 2024**. 1 January is excluded as a public holiday; weekends are excluded by definition.

## Methodology summary

The analysis follows an IMRaD structure (see [`paper/main.tex`](paper/main.tex)):

1. **Baseline reconstruction (Médiane 10J):** for each intra-day 10-minute slot τ, take the median power across the 10 business reference days. This counterfactual is robust to up to four outlier reference days.
2. **Curtailed power per timestep:** `C(t) = max(R(t) − A(t), 0)` where `R` is the baseline and `A` the actual measured power. The non-negativity floor guards against timesteps where the client ramped up rather than down.
3. **Delivered volume:** `E_C^tot = Σ C(t) · Δt` over the 12 timesteps of the window, with `Δt = 1/6 h`.
4. **Realisation rate:** `ρ = E_C^tot / E_C^exp` where `E_C^exp = P_set · T_act = 100 MWh`.
5. **Financial settlement:** remuneration = `E_C^tot × 350 €/MWh`; penalty = `0.35 × deficit × 250 €/MWh` if `ρ < 80%`, else 0.

See `paper/main.tex` Section 2 (Methodology) for full equations and assumptions.

## Key technical decisions

- **Median over mean for the baseline:** a single atypical reference day (unplanned shutdown, maintenance, metering glitch) would drag a mean-based baseline away from the client's true operating point and mechanically penalise the client. The median absorbs up to four such outliers out of 10 without moving.
- **Non-negativity floor on curtailed power:** if `A(t) > R(t)` (the client ramped up rather than down), the timestep contribution is clipped to 0 rather than counted as negative curtailment.
- **Window-level averaging instead of per-timestep enforcement:** the 80% threshold is applied to the total realisation rate, not to each timestep individually. This matches the physical reality of industrial flexibility: a 50 MW reduction on a 200 MW site requires coordination of multiple unit operations and cannot be applied instantaneously.
- **Public holiday handling:** 1 January 2024 is excluded from the reference window. The business-day filter happens to also exclude any prior activations — but this is a coincidence of the present case, not a structural protection (see Discussion §4.2).

## Limitations

The analysis covers **a single activation** on a single client. Three extensions would be operationally relevant:

1. **Uncertainty band on the baseline:** the present framework returns a point estimate of the curtailed volume with no associated error bar. A confidence band (e.g. inter-quartile range across the 10 reference days) would give a more honest estimate at the cost of a more complex settlement rule.

2. **Portfolio-level non-linearity:** the penalty branch is convex below the 80% threshold. A portfolio-level realisation rate of 80% can hide several fully compliant clients and one severely non-compliant site with sharply worse economics than the headline suggests. Portfolio audits should report the realisation distribution, not only its mean.

3. **Baseline gaming:** a client who learns that the baseline is used for settlement has an incentive to inflate consumption on potential reference days (*rebound gaming* / *baseline inflation*). A multi-event analysis over several months would be required to flag systematic deviations between reference-day and non-reference-day consumption for the same client.
